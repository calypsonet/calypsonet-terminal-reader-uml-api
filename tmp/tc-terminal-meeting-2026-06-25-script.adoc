== About this script

This is the speaker script for the *TC Terminal meeting of June 25, 2026*.
It is written to be read out loud. The meeting lasts *2 hours*. *Monetech* will
give a short *15-minute live demo* on slide S7.

*Speaking style*

* Use very short sentences. Stop often.
* Each time you say a new short word (an acronym), say what it means.
* For each topic: first the *why*, then the *what*, then ask a question.
* After each question, *stop and wait*. Silence is fine.

*Slide markers* `[S1] ... [S13]` follow the deck order (`Présentation.pdf`).
*Time* is in minutes. The full table is at the end.

== TOPIC 1 -- Reader Layer Requirements (v2.0 to v3.0P2) -- 15 min

=== [S4] Section opener -- 1 min

The version we look at today is *3.0 Patch 2*. The last version you approved
is *2.0*. That was on September 7, 2023. So I will show you what changed
*between 2.0 and 3.0P2*. As one single set of changes. This is the package
you must approve.

A word on the "P2" name. The document is still under review. *P1* and *P2*
are just two review rounds of the same major step. For today, you can ignore
the "P2". What I will show you is the full 3.0 content as it stands now.

=== [S5] Changes overview -- 10 min

==== Why a new major version

Since version 2.0 was approved in 2023, the *STA certification* has become
required for every CNA-certified terminal. STA stands for *Smart Ticketing
Alliance*. STA now covers many things our Reader Layer document used to
describe. Things like RF behaviour, contactless polling, and presence check.

If we keep saying the same things in *two documents*, we have a problem.
If STA changes a rule and we forget to change ours, the two documents *no
longer agree*. The people who build the terminals do not know which one to
follow. The CNA tests and the STA tests can even ask for opposite things.

So the idea of 3.0 is simple: *what STA already covers, we drop from our
document*. We keep only what is *truly specific* to the Calypso Reader Layer.

==== What changes between v2.0 and v3.0P2

Six rules are *removed*. STA already checks all six. Keeping them was just
saying the same thing twice:

[cols="2,3,3", options="header"]
|===
| Rule (in v2.0) | What it asked for | Now checked by

| `RL-CL-SHUTOFF.1`   | Turn off the RF field                          | STA test *PCD 2.8*
| `RL-CL-PROTOCOL.1`  | Turn each contactless protocol on or off       | No longer needed -- STA sets the baseline
| `RL-CL-POLLING.1`   | Pick the polling scenario                      | STA test *PCD 2.4*
| `RL-DET-PCRQ.1`     | Ask the card if it is still there              | STA test *PCD 2.5*
| `RL-DET-PCAPDU.1`   | Card-presence check by APDU                    | STA presence-check tests
| `RL-DET-PCIBLOCK.1` | Card-presence check by I-Block                 | STA presence-check tests
|===

One rule is *updated*:

* `RL-CL-RFSTA.2` becomes the *single entry point* for all RF rules. Before,
  it pointed to the old standard name (ISO/IEC TS 24192). Now it points
  straight to the *STA Technical Guidelines, version 3.3 or later*. So when
  STA gives us a new version, we follow it without doing anything.

No new rule is added. *The document gets smaller. It does not grow.*

==== Small editorial changes

We also rewrote a few short parts to fit the new, shorter RF section.
No new technical content. Just text cleanup.

==== A wider idea -- not for today

We are also thinking about a bigger move. The idea: *make our Reader Layer
Requirements free from any certification body*. Today we point to STA.
Tomorrow it could be another body. If we keep naming one body in the rules,
we tie our own document to *their update calendar*. We do not want that.

The plan we are studying:

* keep the Reader Layer rules *neutral* -- no certification body named;
* and move all the body-specific text -- like the STA mapping tables --
  into *separate operator documents*.

The patch we show you today (3.0P2) is the *first step*: it removes the
duplicate rules. The full move to a neutral document is *a separate piece of
work*. The TC will decide on it later. Today we only ask you to approve
v3.0P2.

==== Questions for the TC -- three points to confirm

. *The six removals* -- can you confirm they are OK? Is there any system
  where one of these rules was still useful *on its own, without STA*?
. *The update to `RL-CL-RFSTA.2`* -- pointing to STA Technical Guidelines
  3.3 or later -- is this how your test labs already work?
. *The wider idea* -- a neutral document, free from any certification body
  -- do you want it on the agenda of the next meeting? Any worry to share now?

=== [S8] What it is -- the threat and the fix idea -- 15 min

==== Why we must act now

The attack you just saw *works on any contactless terminal today*. The card
does not know it is being relayed. The terminal does not know the card is far
away. Looking at the messages, *everything looks normal*.

Until today, our defences sat in two places. Some were in the *back office*
-- looking for clones in the logs. Others were in the *crypto layer* --
Calypso REV3 stops people from injecting fake data. *None of them catches a
simple spatial relay in real time, at the terminal*. We need a check that
runs *now, locally, at the terminal*.

==== A word on the risk -- it depends on the use case

One word before we go on. *The risk is not the same for every system.*

* For *access control* -- a door, a restricted area, anything where *one
  fake entry already hurts* -- the risk is *high*. The back office sees the
  fraud too late. The local check is your only line.
* For *transit ticketing systems* that already have *strong anti-clone checks
  in the back office*, the risk may be *very small in practice*. Mass fraud
  shows up in the logs. The back office stops it before it spreads. For these
  operators, the relay defence is more an *extra layer of safety* than a
  must-have.

In short: today's proposal *gives every operator the option* to turn this
defence on. We do not force it. Each operator decides, based on what they
think their own risk is.

==== The key idea

A relay attack has one thing it cannot hide: *time*. The attacker must add
a second contactless link, plus a network link to the far-away card. That
takes time. Always. It cannot be made shorter than a physical limit -- about
a few tens of milliseconds.

So if we can *measure* how long a card command takes, and if we know what is
*normal for that card*, we can spot a relay as an *abnormal extra wait*.

==== Which commands can we measure

We need commands where *both the question and the answer are unpredictable*.
If they can be guessed, an attacker can prepare the answer in advance. Then
they avoid the round-trip on the network. And our timing check sees nothing.

Three Calypso commands fit this need:

* *Open Secure Session* -- a random number from the terminal goes in. A
  random number from the card comes out.
* *Close Secure Session* -- random by design of the session.
* *SV operations* -- Debit, Reload, Undebit. Each one carries random data
  in and out. SV stands for *Stored Value*.

These three commands become our *time probes*.

==== Tuning per card family

A Calypso card from one chip family is not as fast as one from another. The
time depends on the chip and the software inside. So we cannot use *one
single time limit* for the whole network. If we did, we would either block
slow but honest cards, or let the relay pass on a fast card.

The fix: *split the card fleet into groups* and give *each group its own
time limit*.

==== Questions for the TC

Two questions before we look at how to set this up:

. The idea -- measure, compare, stop if too long -- does it fit the risk you
  face on your own network?
. Are there other commands you would also want to time? Or do OSS, CSS and
  SV cover all the sensitive ones for you?

=== [S9] Setup challenges -- how to identify each card -- 15 min

==== Why this slide matters

The idea is clear. But *the setup is the hard part*. Each time a card
touches the reader, the terminal must answer one question:

[quote]
____
This card I just saw -- which time group does it belong to?
____

If the terminal cannot tell the card apart, it cannot pick the right time
limit. So we need a way to *identify* the card.

Two ways have been looked at. Each has good and bad sides. We need your view
on which one -- or both -- to put in the standard.

==== Way 1 -- by Serial Number range (CSN ranges)

The operator says: _"cards with a serial number between A and B follow time
profile P1, cards between B and C follow P2"_, and so on. The setup is done
by giving a *lower limit* for each range -- we call it `csnMin`.

* *Which cards.* This way works best for *older REV1 and REV2 cards*. On
  those cards, the startup bytes were often *changed on purpose to look
  like older products* (we call that emulation). So we cannot trust the
  startup data. The serial number is the only safe key.
* *Good side:* very precise for *closed systems* -- when the operator knows
  exactly which cards it has put in the field.
* *Bad side:* serial number ranges *do not always come as one big block*.
  A maker can split them by factory, by customer, by chip type. Each new
  batch can use new numbers. Keeping that map up to date in the field is
  *a lot of work*.

This is the worry Stéphane raised on the forum.

==== Way 2 -- by Startup Information

Each Calypso card sends back four short bytes. These bytes describe the
*product*: Platform, Software Issuer, Software Version, Software Revision.
We can use these four bytes as the *group key*, instead of the serial number.

* *Which cards.* This way works for *modern Calypso products*: *Prime*,
  *Light*, *Basic*, and *HCE* on mobile phones. (HCE stands for *Host Card
  Emulation*.) On these cards, the startup data is stable and tells you the
  real product.
* *Good side:* much simpler map. The same four bytes mean the same chip
  family, every time. *Terminal vendors could even keep this map* -- so
  each operator does not have to.
* *Bad side:* these four bytes are sent back *outside the secure session*.
  So on a fake or relayed card, an attacker could lie about them. To be safe,
  we re-read them *inside the secure session* -- using a special command
  (Get Data on tag 6F). This only works from *Prime Revision 3 onwards*.

Pierre's reply on the forum says it well:

* *REV1 and REV2 cards* -- use the serial number. Startup data can be faked
  (emulation).
* *REV3 and later -- Prime, Light, Basic, HCE* -- use the startup data,
  inside the session.

==== Where we want to go

The final API will most likely support *both ways*. The operator picks the
one that fits its own card mix.

==== Questions for the TC

. Do you agree that *both ways* should be in the standard?
. Operators in the room -- which way works for *your* system today?
. Terminal vendors -- could you keep a *startup-info to time-profile* map
  as a vendor service?

== TOPIC 3 -- Terminal APIs 3.0 -- 45 min

=== [S10] Section opener -- 1 min

The third topic is the big new release of the Terminal APIs. Version 3.0.

This release is *for approval*. Today we do not look at Java code or UML
class diagrams. We focus on the *why* of each change. The full change
document and the UML diagrams are linked from the forum.

=== [S11] Scope, drivers, migration support -- 10 min

==== Why a major version, and why now

Three things pushed for it at the same time:

. A new CNA product, *OpenSAM*. To work, OpenSAM needs *many application
  channels open at the same time* on one SAM. Today's APIs assume only one
  at a time. We cannot fit OpenSAM in without breaking that.
. *Apple ECP* -- *Enhanced Contactless Polling*. This is the special polling
  frame Apple asks for, to make Calypso work in transit mode on the iPhone.
  Our 2.x API has no clean place for it.
. The *relay attack defence* we just talked about needs to *measure and cap*
  the time of each card exchange. We have to add this to our request and
  response objects.

A major version is also the right moment for *cleanup*. We can drop every
old, marked-for-removal item we kept since 1.0. We take that chance in 3.0.

==== Scope of the release

Seven APIs change at the same time:

* The *three current APIs* move to 3.0.0: Reader API, Card API (internal),
  Calypso Card API.
* One *new base API* is created: the Terminal Definitions API, version 1.0.0.
* Three *side APIs* move to 2.0.0 at the same time: Legacy SAM, Generic Card,
  Storage Card.

==== On the integrator side -- a step-by-step migration

We want to be reassuring on this point. Yes, 3.0 breaks some application code.
That is what a major version means. But the migration is *simple, known in
advance, and step by step*.

A *full migration guide* will come out with the release. It is written with
one goal in mind: *no integrator should have to guess what changed or why*.
The guide gives you:

* a *one-to-one map* for every renamed, refactored or removed item. You read
  the table, you apply the change.
* *before / after code snippets* for the common cases (selection, observation,
  transactions, multi-channel access, exceptions). You copy the new shape.
* *short recipes* for typical code. A junior developer can follow them with
  no big decision to make.
* a *known-pitfalls list* -- the small things that catch people the first
  time.

In real life, most of the work is *find and replace*, plus a few local
rewrites. Nothing odd. *Your code structure does not have to change.*

==== Questions for the TC

. Does this *step-by-step migration plan* -- one full guide instead of a
  long deprecation phase -- match what your developer teams need?
. Is there any integrator with a *special concern* on a part of their code
  that a simple guide would not cover? We want to find these cases now, and
  add them to the guide.

=== [S12] The seven themes -- 30 min (about 4 min each)

I will now go through the seven themes one by one. For each one, I will say:

* *Why* we did it,
* *What* the application sees,
* And then I ask: any worry from the TC?

==== Theme 1 -- Multiple logical channels -- 5 min

*Why.* The OpenSAM is able to hold *several security apps, all open at the
same time*, each on its own channel. Today's API cannot describe that.

*What changes.* Many card apps can now be picked and used *at the same
time*, each on its own channel. Each card *knows on which channel it is*
and *if it is still alive*. The framework gives a clean way to ask:
_"Is my card still active? On which channel?"_

*Question.* Any worry about multi-channel? Any system that would break
because of this change?

==== Theme 2 -- Relay-attack defence -- 4 min

*Why.* This is the API side of the proposal from Topic 2. The framework
needs a way to *send a max time with each request*, and *send back the
measured time with each response*.

*What changes.* Two layers:

* In the *Card API*: each APDU exchange can now carry a max time. A special
  exception fires if the time goes over.
* In the *Calypso Card API*: you can set time caps for full secure sessions
  and for SV operations. With a `csnMin` filter -- so the cap can be made
  stricter for newer cards, as we talked about.

*Question.* This puts the Topic 2 proposal straight into the API. Does the
TC agree on both layers -- per-APDU and per-session?

==== Theme 3 -- Simpler observation -- 3 min

*Why.* In 2.x, starting card detection took many small steps. Register an
observer. Register an error handler. Then call start. Some halfway states
were not valid. In real life, every integrator we asked registered exactly
*one* observer.

*What changes.* Starting card detection, setting it up, and registering
*one* listener for events *and* errors -- all in *one call*. No halfway
state. Smaller API.

*Question.* Anyone here using more than one observer today? If so, please
speak up.

==== Theme 4 -- See the secure session state -- 3 min

*Why.* Until 2.x, the application had no way to ask:
_"Is a secure session open right now?"_. It had to track the state on its
own side, in parallel. That is easy to get wrong.

*What changes.* The application can now ask, any time: is a Calypso secure
session open? What kind is it -- symmetric or asymmetric (PKI)? Which write
level was asked for? The answer is a *snapshot* -- a copy that does not
change later.

*Question.* Does this match what your business code needs? Is anything
missing from the snapshot?

==== Theme 5 -- Strong types for RF and card types -- 4 min

*Why.* In 1.x and 2.x, you set up protocols with *free text strings*:
`"ISO_14443_4"`, `"FELICA"`, and so on. No two integrators used the exact
same strings. Typos went through the compiler. There was no central list of
valid values. And there was no clean place to set the *Apple ECP* polling
frame.

*What changes.* The strings go away. Two strong-typed lists take their
place: `RfTechnology` for the radio layer, `CardType` for the detected
product. Now the compiler catches typos. The IDE shows you what is valid.
The Apple ECP frame fits naturally into the detection settings.

*Question.* The starting content of the two lists is in the change document.
Two specific points need your green light:

* We *merge ISO 14443-A and ISO 14443-B* into one value `ISO_14443_4` at the
  transport level. At the product level we still tell A and B apart. Does
  this match how you model things?
* Is any value missing from `RfTechnology` or `CardType` that you need from
  day one?

==== Theme 6 -- New Definitions API -- 3 min

*Why.* The two lists from Theme 5 do not belong to the Reader API alone.
They are *shared* between several APIs. If we put them in the Reader API,
every other API that wants to use them must depend on the full Reader API.
That is too much. So we move them out, into a *new base module*.

*What changes.* A new repository, `calypsonet-terminal-definitions-uml-api`,
version 1.0.0. A new Java module, `keypop-definitions-jvm-api`. The Reader
API now depends on it. Other Terminal APIs may depend on it later.

*Question.* Do you agree with the idea of a separate base API, instead of
a sub-package inside the Reader API?

==== Theme 7 -- Command identification -- 3 min

*Why.* On Generic Card and Storage Card, the application *prepares many
commands*, then sends them all together. Today, if one fails, the
application cannot easily tell *which one* in the group failed.

*What changes.* When you prepare a command, you can now tag it with *your
own number*. If something fails, you get back the number of the broken
command. If all is fine, you can also pick one response by its number.

*Question.* Any worry about this design -- a simple number given by the
application? Do you need another kind of id?

==== Cross-cutting cleanup -- 2 min

On top of the seven themes, version 3.0 also drops:

* *All deprecated items* kept since 1.0.
* The `ChannelControl` enum. It is replaced by clear channel-close methods.
* Some *duplicate exceptions* on the Calypso and SAM side. They were just
  copies of Reader-level ones.

*Question.* Is any TC member using one of these deprecated items today?
Please speak up now.
